# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Gamers who want a focused place to share and discover gaming posts. They come to MyTuums to publish short text posts, optionally attach gaming-related media, follow other users, browse by game and profile, and participate through lightweight social actions without getting pulled into a clips-first, streaming-first, or chat-first product.

## Product Purpose

MyTuums exists to give gaming conversation a cleaner, more focused home than a generic social feed. Success means users can quickly create a handle-first identity, post gaming thoughts or media, discover relevant posts through feeds and game pages, and interact without the product feeling noisy, algorithm-heavy, or overbuilt.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, welcoming, focused.

The voice should feel clear, modern, and confident without becoming cold. It should feel friendly to gamers without leaning on loud esports theatrics or novelty-first "gamer" styling. The emotional goal is fast trust: users should feel like they understand the product quickly and want to participate right away.

## Anti-references

Do not make the UI look too similar to Twitter/X.

Avoid the familiar text-wall timeline look, divider-heavy feed sameness, and generic social-media scaffolding that makes a product feel like a reskin of an existing mainstream feed. MyTuums should feel like its own product, not a visual echo of Twitter/X.

## Design Principles

- Familiar product patterns, distinct execution. Use trustworthy app conventions, but shape them into a visual identity that does not read as a Twitter/X clone.
- Gaming-native, not gamer-cliche. Support gaming culture and media without defaulting to neon overload, edgy esports tropes, or streamer-chaos styling.
- Posting should feel lightweight. The path from browsing to posting to engagement should stay fast, legible, and low-friction.
- Identity is handle-first. Profiles, usernames, favorite games, and authored posts should make people feel recognizable without requiring real-name conventions or creator-brand theatrics.
- Clarity over feed chaos. Keep hierarchy, navigation, and moderation cues readable so the app feels focused rather than loud.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet the repo baseline: keyboard-first navigation, visible focus states, reduced-motion support, semantic controls, and WCAG AA color contrast where practical. Accessibility should be treated as a core product quality bar, not as a pass added after visual polish.
